Input datasets

Source data and integration problem

Two movie datasets capture complementary aspects of the same domain; the first design decision is how to integrate them without losing source-specific meaning.

IMDb data

  • Movies are identified by the unique tconst key (e.g. tt0111161).
  • Ratings, votes, people, crew and principal roles.
  • Strong coverage for movie identity and audience evaluation; used as the reference for ratings and roles.

Kaggle movie metadata

  • TMDB identifiers and production metadata.
  • Budget, revenue, countries, languages and companies.
  • Useful for financial and production-oriented analysis; used to complete what IMDb does not provide.

Without reconciliation, ratings and financials could not be analyzed together.

Explain why reconciliation is necessary before any meaningful analytical model can be built: IMDb alone has no full financial picture, while Kaggle alone lacks the same rating and role precision.

Dimensional Fact Model

Fact granularity and dimensions

The DFM fixes the analytical grain before the physical star is built; that choice governs every OLAP aggregation.

1
1

Choose analysis axes

  • Film is degenerate; Time is year → decade → era; day, month and quarter are unused stubs (=1).
  • Genre: genre → genre group.
  • ProductionContext branches to company and country → continent.
  • Director is flat; language → language family. Both are optional.
2
2

Define the fact grain

  • Grain: film × genre × production context × director × original language.
  • Genre, production context and director are multiple dimensions.
  • One film → multiple fact rows.
3
3

Attach movie measures

  • Revenue, budget and ROI from Kaggle.
  • Average rating and vote counts from IMDb.
  • Deduplicate Film before SUM / AVG; never SUM ROI or rating.

Genre, ProductionContext and Director create fan-out. Director and original language are optional; original language is single and does not multiply rows.

Explain that production country, director and original language are optional axes. The fact keeps the full multi-valued grain, so OLAP queries deduplicate to their target level.

Aggregation issue

Deduplication prevents inflated totals

Why the bias appears

  • Genres, company-country contexts and directors are M:N — they fan-out fact rows.
  • Original language is single and optional, so it does not create fan-out.
  • Revenue and budget are movie-level measures.
  • Summing across fan-out rows inflates those values.
!
!

How analytical validity is preserved

  • Use a CTE to deduplicate at the target dimensional level.
  • Aggregate only after deduplication.
  • Apply consistently: results must reflect real data, not modeling artifacts.

Evaluation

What the model supports — and where it stops

Stable invariants protect the model; documented source limits define the boundary of interpretation.

What is demonstrated

  • Reconciled and warehouse layers run in PostgreSQL; raw sources stay files, and OLAP queries run over the warehouse.
  • Stable integrity checks block invalid states.
  • Canonical exports connect every query to its visual output.

Documented limitations

  • Director nationality is unavailable in IMDb.
  • Production geography is film context, not company domicile.
  • Budget coverage limits the financial subset.
Distinguish volatile snapshot counts from stable blocking invariants. State the production-context and director-nationality limitations without weakening the validated model.

Three takeaways

Reliable analysis starts before the query

Reconcile identity

Join complementary sources through auditable IDs.

Fix the grain

Define what one fact row represents before aggregation.

Deduplicate first

Aggregate movie-level measures only at the target analytical level.

Conclude the scope of the work: data integration, dimensional modeling and analytical querying are all covered in a single pipeline.
